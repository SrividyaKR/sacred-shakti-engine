# Tripura Sundari / Shodashi (Mahavidya 3): research dossier

Status: **draft for human review**. Researched 2026-10-08 from ~14 searches and 8 opened pages. Labels: **[scriptural]** = tied to a named text; **[devotional]** = popular or temple tradition; **[interpretive]** = a commentator's or website's reading. Many web sources are devotional sites; the strongest are the Sahasranama text page, the scholarly brhat.in article, and Wikipedia (which carries quality banners). Where sources disagree, both are given.

## Who she is

- Third of the ten Mahavidyas; the central goddess of the **Sri Vidya** tradition. Also called **Lalita** ("the playful / lovely one"), **Rajarajeshwari** (queen of queens), **Kameshwari/Kamakshi** (goddess of love / "love-eyed"), **Sri Mata** (Wikipedia: [Tripura Sundari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura_Sundari)). Sources treat Lalita and Tripura Sundari as the same goddess; the Lalitopakhyana presents Lalita as Tripura's "fullest form" in the war narrative ([brhat.in](https://www.brhat.in/dhiti/the-goddess-tripura-and-the-tripurasundari-tradition)).
- **Name "Tripura Sundari":** glossed "the Beauty of the Three Worlds" ([brhat.in](https://www.brhat.in/dhiti/the-goddess-tripura-and-the-tripurasundari-tradition)). Wikipedia notes one reading of "Tripura" from three clusters of letters in her mantra. A bookseller summary of the *Tripura Rahasya* reads the "three cities" as waking, dreaming and deep sleep, with her as the consciousness behind all three ([interpretive](https://www.exoticindia.com/book/details/tripura-rahasya-mystery-of-goddess-tripura-section-on-wisdom-jnana-khanda-hbt155)). The brhat.in article itself gives no "three cities" explanation.
- **Tone of the tradition:** Srividya sees her as "benign and beautiful", in deliberate contrast to the fierce Kali-kula goddesses (Wikipedia). This is the sharp break from Kali and Tara.
- **Purpose for devotees:** beauty and auspiciousness (the tradition is called *saubhagya*, "good fortune"; brhat.in), worldly fulfilment and liberation together (Sri Vidya's stated aim), mastery of desire and mind (the bow/arrow/noose/goad readings below), and knowledge of the self as consciousness (*Tripura Rahasya*).
- **Texts:** *Lalita Sahasranama* (1000 names; presented inside the *Brahmanda Purana*), *Lalitopakhyana* (the Lalita story, the 4th book of the *Brahmanda Purana*; 32 or 40 chapters depending on source), *Tripura Rahasya* (nondual philosophy; Dattatreya teaching Parashurama; date 11th to 17th century by Wikipedia, ~10th century by another source), *Saundarya Lahari* (100 verses attributed to Adi Shankara; authorship disputed), *Nityashodashikarnava* and *Yoginihridaya* (11th-century Kashmir tantras), *Tripura Upanishad*, *Mantramahodadhi* (1589, an iconography manual) (sources: [brhat.in](https://www.brhat.in/dhiti/the-goddess-tripura-and-the-tripurasundari-tradition), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura_Sundari), [Tripura Rahasya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura_Rahasya)).

## Appearance (what is consistent, what varies)

### Consistent across sources
- **Four arms** holding a **noose**, a **goad** (elephant hook), a **sugarcane bow** and **flower arrows**. [scriptural] Sahasranama opening verses (names 2 to 3: "four-armed", "noose of attachment", "bow of sugarcane which is the mind", "arrows which are the five subtle elements"); dhyana verse on the same page ([Lalita Sahasranama text and meaning](https://www.radhakrishnatemple.net/blog/lalitha-sahasranamam-lyrics-and-meaning-2/)). Also [Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html), [brhat.in](https://www.brhat.in/dhiti/the-goddess-tripura-and-the-tripurasundari-tradition).
- **Radiance like a thousand rising suns** (*udyadbhanu sahasrabha*, Sahasranama verse 2) and a **red glow that fills the universe** (verse 3). [scriptural]
- **Three eyes**, jewelled, a **crescent moon on the crown or forehead** (Tripura Rahasya per brhat.in; Sahasranama verse 5 compares the forehead to the eighth-day moon; Vedadhara also gives crescent moon) ([Vedadhara](https://www.vedadhara.com/tripura-sundari)).
- **Gentle smile**, and the face is the point of the iconography. Sahasranama verses 5 to 11 describe the face feature by feature: moon-like brow with a dark musk mark, darting fish-shaped eyes, nose ornament brighter than stars, kadamba-flower earrings (one sun, one moon), ruby-bright cheeks, coral lips, sweet speech "surpassing the veena", and a smile that captivates Kameshwara. [scriptural] (radhakrishnatemple.net page above). Hair fragrant with champaka, ashoka and punnaga flowers (verse 4).
- **Youth:** "often depicted as a 16-year-old girl" (Wikipedia), hence the name Shodashi ("sixteen").
- **Seated on a lotus** set on a throne. [scriptural/devotional, see variation below.]

### Varies between sources
- **Complexion:** "dusky, red or golden" ([Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html)); "glowing red" (Vedadhara); "crimson" (Sahasranama dhyana); "vermilion-red" (first dhyana verse on the Sahasranama page); the *Tripura Rahasya* also gives a **golden, eighteen-armed martial form** (brhat.in). Red is the commonest.
- **Number of arrows:** five (Sahasranama; Drik Panchang; five tanmatras/sense objects) versus three (one Tibetan-art page describing a bow and three arrows; this may be Kurukulla-influenced, see [tibetanart.com](https://tibetanart.com/artworks/358-mahavidya-tripura-sundari)).
- **Hands:** the usual four-object set versus the **peaceful gestures** (*abhaya*, no fear; *varada*, granting wishes) which appear in images from the 12th century on; Mahidhara's 1589 manual records bow and arrows replacing those gestures (brhat.in). One dhyana verse instead gives a wine-cup and red lotus; another gives a golden lotus with fearlessness and wealth gestures (Sahasranama page, dhyana verses 1 and 3).
- **Garments:** red (Drik Panchang, Sahasranama dhyana verse 4 "crimson, glowing like hibiscus"), or yellow (dhyana verse 3). No source describes a tiger skin or skull garland for the Lalita form.
- **Mount:** a lion (Wikipedia infobox) versus a bull and tiger skin, snake garland, trident and drum (Vamakeshvara Tantra, Wikipedia, tagged "citation needed", so a different, fierce iconography). Treat the fierce description as unreliable.
- **Companions at her throne:** Lakshmi and Saraswati attend her (one gallery description of a dhyana-stuti; see [brhat.in search result](https://www.brhat.in/dhiti/the-goddess-tripura-and-the-tripurasundari-tradition) and Lalitopakhyana's tradition that Sarasvati, Lakshmi and Gauri worship Lalita). Secondary only.

### The throne (disagreement)
- **Devotional/popular:** a throne whose four legs are Brahma, Vishnu, Rudra and Ishana, with **Sadashiva as the seat (or lying beneath the lotus)**; the meaning given is that even the great gods are powerless without her ([Vedadhara](https://www.vedadhara.com/tripura-sundari); [Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura_Sundari)).
- **Scriptural:** *Tripura Rahasya* (Jnana Khanda ch. 20, vv. 36-37 per Wikipedia) has a four-legged platform (Brahma, Vishnu, Mahesha, Ishwara) with Sadashiva as its back, in a Chintamani (wish-jewel) mansion in a Kadamba grove. Sahasranama name 249 says the legs are "Pancha Brahmas" (five), which does not match.
- Visual note: the throne-of-corpses image is a posture, not gore, but it is the one element we should avoid or abstract (a plain lotus throne is sufficient and is also in sources).

## Settings and scenes

- **Rising-sun red light is core, not incidental.** The Sahasranama text itself says "radiance of a thousand rising suns" and "red glow filling the universe". One source places her "standing within the sun's orbit" ([hindu-blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2025/01/symbolism-in-image-of-goddess-shodashi.html), unnamed sources). **Conflict with the series: night only, no daylight vocabulary.** See "Conflicts with series rules" below.
- **Home:** the **Sri Chakra** (Sri Yantra) is "the other body of the deity" (brhat.in; Yoginihridaya calls it her *vapus*, body). Nine interlocking triangles, four pointing up and five down, around a central dot (*bindu*) (Wikipedia). Worship is Sri Chakra puja (Vedadhara).
- **Her city and palace:** In the Lalitopakhyana (devotional/scriptural retellings), Lalita lives in **Sri Nagara**, with a Chintamani mansion in a kadamba grove (Tripura Rahasya via Wikipedia). Her chariot is called Chakra Raja Ratha ([indianetzone](https://www.indianetzone.com/legend_goddess_lalita)). Details of the city were not independently verified.
- **Fire-pit of consciousness** (*Chidagnikunda*): her emergence scene (see stories).

## Named forms

- **Lalita:** the sovereign form who fights Bhandasura.
- **Bala Tripurasundari (Bala):** the child form and daughter; in popular accounts of the Lalitopakhyana she kills Bhandasura's thirty sons ([realshepower](https://www.realshepower.in/the-story-of-bala-tripura-sundari/), [indianetzone](https://www.indianetzone.com/legend_goddess_lalita)). Device: a different, child-like look; do not conflate with the 16-year-old.
- **Shodashi:** the 16-year-old form, and the sixteen-syllable mantra deity.
- **Kamakshi / Kameshwari:** "love-eyed". Hayagriva says in the Lalitopakhyana that Lalita reappeared as Kamakshi (via kamakotimandali, secondary).
- **Rajarajeshwari:** the queen of queens form.
- **Tripura Matabari image:** the Udaipur (Tripura) Tripura Sundari is identified as the Shakti of the Tripura peetha (see temples).

## The meaning of "sixteen" (Shodashi)

Three readings, all must be kept separate:
1. **Sixteen years old:** a young girl at full beauty. Popular and Wikipedia ([Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html); Vedadhara says she "appeared as a 16-year-old" from the sacrificial fire).
2. **Sixteen-syllable mantra:** the 15-syllable *Panchadashi* mantra becomes the 16-syllable Shodashi when one seed syllable (*Shrim*) is added; Tripurasundari with her 15 Nityas (lunar-day goddesses) presides ([kamakotimandali on Srividya](https://www.kamakotimandali.com/?p=2588), as summarised by search). One commentator, Sharma, denied that a separate Shodashi vidya exists; the article's author disagrees.
3. **The sixteenth phase of the moon:** in Srividya the sixteenth lunar digit is Shodashi/Maha-Tripura-Sundari, represented by the central bindu of the Sri Chakra; the Nityas map to the lunar days and the full moon is their culmination ([Advaita-l thread on the 16 kalas](https://lists.advaita-vedanta.org/archives/advaita-l/2008-January/019719.html), a scholarly mailing-list discussion; interpretive).
- Also popular (unsourced, interpretive): "embodies sixteen types of desires" ([Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html)). I found no text for this.
- The Sri Chakra's second enclosure is a sixteen-petalled lotus (search summary of a Dikshitar navavarana page; not otherwise verified).

## Symbolism of the held objects (interpretive)

- Noose = attachment; goad = repulsion/control and guidance; bow of sugarcane = the mind; five flower arrows = the five sense objects (or five subtle elements) ([Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html), Sahasranama page).
- Amritananda reads noose, goad and bow-and-arrow as the powers of **will, knowledge and action** (brhat.in).
- Vedadhara notes the bow and arrows are Kamadeva's (the god of love's) weapons: she holds the god of love's own tools.

## Stories

1. **The fire-pit birth and the war on Bhandasura** (scriptural: *Lalitopakhyana*, *Brahmanda Purana*; popular retellings agree on the outline).
   - Shiva burned Kama (the god of love) to ashes. From the ashes arose the demon **Bhandasura**, who drove the gods from heaven. The gods performed a great fire sacrifice, and **Lalita rose out of the Chidagni Kunda** ("fire-pit of pure awareness") on a chariot, in the form of a sixteen-year-old (Vedadhara). Shiva appears beside her as Kameshwara. She leads the war; the climax is Bhandasura slain by a missile called Mahakameshwara after a four-day battle ([Wikipedia: Bhandasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhandasura), [Vedadhara](https://www.vedadhara.com/tripura-sundari), [indianetzone](https://www.indianetzone.com/legend_goddess_lalita)). Her child form Bala kills Bhandasura's sons; Matangi and Varahi deal with his brothers (indianetzone; popular).
   - Sources differ on whether the gods approach Shiva first, who tells them only Lalita can help, or pray to Devi directly (indianetzone vs Vedadhara). The theological reading is that the birth is the Absolute's self-luminous power taking form, not a physical birth (one baby-name site, hindutone; weak source, interpretive only).
   - Keep the war non-graphic: the sources give her calm sovereignty, not a fight scene, as the useful material.
2. **Lalita revives Kama** (scriptural: *Lalitopakhyana*, chapter 30 titled the resurrection of Madana, per search summary of [hindupedia](https://www.hindupedia.com/en/Lalitop%C4%81khy%C4%81na) and [Wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/lalitopakhyana-lalita-mahatmya/d/doc362969.html)). Rati, wife of the burned Kama, is presented to Lalita; out of compassion she restores him. One telling: Lalita smiles with compassion and looks at Kameshwara, and from her glance Manmatha is reborn (secondary; [kanchikamakshi.org](https://kanchikamakshi.org/?p=505) among results). This differs from the Bhagavata/Kalidasa versions, where Rati pleads to Parvati or a voice promises revival after Shiva's marriage ([hindu-blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/11/story-shiva-burning-kamadeva.html)). **This is the strongest "face does the work" story: a compassionate smile and glance that restores life.**
3. **Origin of the thousand names** (devotional/scriptural framing). Agastya asks Hayagriva for the thousand names; Hayagriva had held them secret, then reveals them, saying they were composed by eight speech goddesses (Vashini and companions) at Lalita's own command ([search summary, Tirumeeyachur sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirumeeyachur_Mehanadhar_Temple)). At Tirumeeyachur (Tamil Nadu), the local tradition is that Agastya and his wife Lopamudra chanted before Lalithambika and she appeared to them; sources disagree on the site (Kanchipuram is an alternative).
4. **Kamakshi and the sand lingam (Kanchipuram), devotional.** Parvati does penance at Kanchipuram, makes a sand lingam under a mango tree; when the river floods, she embraces the lingam to protect it. This is Parvati's story, linked to Kamakshi. Hayagriva's statement that Lalita is Kamakshi connects it only loosely. Do not present it as a Tripura Sundari legend without a caveat.
5. **Tripura Matabari founding legend (Tripura state), devotional.** King Dhanya Manikya, building a Vishnu temple, is told in a dream to install Tripura Sundari instead, and to fetch the image from near Chittagong in a single night; at the first rays of the sun the image became fixed to the ground where the temple stands. A variant says it was found under the waters of a river. Dated 1501 by some sources ([sacredsites.com](https://sacredsites.com/asia/india/tripura_sundari.html), [greenmesg.org](https://greenmesg.org/bharatavarsha/pilgrimages/tripura/udaipur-matabari_tripurasundari.php)).
6. **Wider Tripura tradition (scholarly, brhat.in):** Tripura appears to Kama, who meditates on her and composes hymns; she is reborn as Nanda's daughter to empower Krishna against Kamsa; as Katyayani and Chandika she slays Shumbha and Nishumbha; Durga as Mahishasuramardini is a partial emanation of Tripura ([brhat.in](https://www.brhat.in/dhiti/the-goddess-tripura-and-the-tripurasundari-tradition)). Shows she is not only the gentle form in the texts.
7. **Tripura Rahasya framing (scriptural):** the teaching comes in conversation. Queen Hemaprabha instructs her husband King Hemachuda about the self; a woman renunciant debates the sage Ashtaka; the goddess herself speaks last (search summary of the Jnana Khanda; secondary). The text identifies Tripura with pure consciousness (*Chiti*).

## Temples, places and regional legends

- **Tripura Sundari Temple, Matabari, Udaipur (Tripura):** a Shakti Peetha; the Peetha Nirnaya Tantra names it as the place Sati's **right foot** fell, with the goddess Tripurasundari and Bhairava Tripuresha. Called Kurma Peeth (tortoise-shaped hill), beside the Kalyan Sagar lake with large fish and tortoises ([greenmesg.org](https://greenmesg.org/bharatavarsha/pilgrimages/tripura/udaipur-matabari_tripurasundari.php), [sacredsites.com](https://sacredsites.com/asia/india/tripura_sundari.html)). The foot-fall claim is devotional/tantric; the founding date varies (1501; repair 1681).
- **Tirumeeyachur (Tamil Nadu), Lalithambigai temple:** linked to the Sahasranama's recitation by Agastya (Wikipedia: [Tirumeeyachur Mehanadhar Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirumeeyachur_Mehanadhar_Temple)). The temple tradition also says the sun god worshipped there to recover his glow; I found this only in a temple site summary, not the Lalita story.
- **Kanchipuram (Kamakshi) and Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham:** Kamakshi is held to be Lalita; the Kanchi and Sringeri Shankaracharya lineages reframed Srividya through Advaita (brhat.in).
- **Kamakhya (Assam):** Tripurasundari, Matangi and Kamala are inside the main complex; the other seven Mahavidyas have separate shrines ([hindutone Kamakhya guide](https://hindutone.com/temples/kamakhya-temple-guide/), quality moderate). Ambubachi Mela is the temple's best-known festival, but it concerns Kamakhya as the goddess; do not attribute it to Tripura Sundari alone.
- Others (Wikipedia, several tagged "citation needed"): Adyar Kamakshi (Chennai), Rajarajeswari (Karnataka), Ratneshwari (Jharkhand).

## Saints and devotees

- **Adi Shankara** is credited with the Saundarya Lahari, a hymn "from her hair to her feet" (Wikipedia). Authorship disputed; a popular legend: Shiva gave Shankara a 100-verse manuscript on Kailash, Nandi tore it in two, and Shiva told him to keep 41 verses and compose the remaining 59 (search summary of [Saundarya Lahari sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saundarya_Lahari); devotional).
- **Muthuswami Dikshitar (1775 to 1835)** composed the Kamalamba Navavarana kritis, one for each enclosure of the Sri Chakra, moving from the outer enclosure inward to the bindu. A related episode: he could not afford the day's offering at home, a student offered to pledge her jewels, he declined, and soon two cartloads of provisions arrived ([rasikas.org Navavarana article](https://www.rasikas.org/forums/viewtopic.php?p=165617), forum compilation, but it cites the story). Kamalamba here is the goddess of Tiruvarur and is identified with Lalita in the Srividya reading; I did not verify that identity beyond the song-cycle's own claim.
- **Bhaskararaya** (18th-century commentator on the Sahasranama): I could not find a source on him in this research round; see Not found.

## Festivals and practice

- **Lalita/Shodashi Jayanti:** Magha Purnima (full moon of the month of Magha) is the most repeated date ([Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html), [hindupad](https://hindupad.com/lalita-jayanti-2013-shodashi-mahavidya-jayanti/)). **Older or regional pages say Margashirsha or Phalguna full moon.** The 2026 Magha date was 1 February 2026 (hindupad).
- **Lalita Panchami:** the fifth day of Sharad Navaratri (Ashvin Shukla Panchami), a fast popular in Gujarat and parts of Maharashtra; practices include reciting the Lalita Sahasranama, the Lalitopakhyana and the Trishati ([bhaktibharat](https://www.bhaktibharat.com/en/festival/lalita-panchami)). Date for 2026 given as 15 October 2026 (one source; verify locally).
- **Gupta Navaratri** is cited as a general Mahavidya observance, but no source explicitly ties Lalita to it.
- **Core practice:** Sri Chakra puja; recitation of the Sahasranama; the Panchadashi and Shodashi mantras (**Om Aim Hreem Shreem Tripura Sundariyai Namah** as a short form; [Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/hindu-goddesses/parvati/mahavidya/shodashi/goddess-shodashi.html)). Friday is her day (Wikipedia infobox).

## Relationships and companions

- **Kameshwara (Shiva as the lord of desire):** her consort; she created him from within herself in one account (indianetzone/hindutone); not a subordinate one: the throne imagery puts Sadashiva beneath her.
- **Kama and Rati:** Kama's weapons are in her hands; she revives him (story 2).
- **Bala:** her daughter in Lalitopakhyana-based retellings.
- **Mantrini (Shyama/Matangi) and Dandini (Varahi):** her war ministers in the Lalitopakhyana tradition; Matangi and Varahi appear in the war in popular retellings (indianetzone), and both are other goddesses of this series (Matangi is the ninth Mahavidya).
- **Lakshmi and Saraswati:** attendants (secondary).
- **Fifteen Nityas:** lunar-day goddesses forming the fifteen-syllable mantra, with her as the sixteenth (kamakotimandali via search).
- **Eight speech goddesses (Vashini and others):** composers of the Sahasranama.

## Conflicts with the series rules (for the human director)

1. **Night only / no daylight vocabulary.** The classical iconography is built on the **rising sun**: radiance of a thousand rising suns, a red glow that fills the universe, a red or "sun-hued" goddess. This is textual (Sahasranama verses 2 and 3), not a modern gloss. There is no classical night scene for her. Options for the human: (a) keep the night rule and use the equally textual **moon** imagery (crescent crown; brow like the eighth-day moon; sixteenth lunar digit as Shodashi; Magha Purnima full-moon jayanti; Sri Chakra bindu as sixteenth kala), with her **own red radiance as the only light source**; (b) allow a pre-dawn or first-light exception for her; (c) use the fire-pit birth, a ritual fire in darkness, which fits night (but the Director's anchor for Kali excludes `fire`, so this is per-anchor). This is a decision, not a research finding. The Semantic Guard blocks "sunrise" and "daylight" words; "rising sun" cannot be written in a prompt as-is.
2. **Fire-pit scene.** Kali's anchor excludes fire; Tripura Sundari's may not. Check her anchor's `environment.excluded`.
3. **Held objects.** The defining iconography (noose, goad, sugarcane bow, flower arrows in four hands) is exactly what the video model renders badly (hands, small tracked objects). The rules say avoid handled objects. The face, crown and smile are the safe, textual centre (Sahasranama verses 5 to 11). The abhaya/varada hand gestures are also attested (12th century on) but hands are still risky. Four arms may drift.
4. **Throne of corpses.** A common image but avoid or abstract; a lotus seat alone is sourced.
5. **Anchor suitability.** Her expression is "gentle smile", "eyes full of compassion", unlike Kali's and Tara's fierce gaze. The Director's stationary_command or atmospheric_arc (eye-level arc around a composed figure) suits sovereignty; a stride does not match any source.
6. **Mantra and names** are for the dossier only: never in video prompts.

## Story seeds (each must name one in the concept)

1. **Rising from the fire-pit of awareness.** The closed or lowered eyes open slowly and the gaze settles on the viewer, as if the goddess has just come into being from the sacrificial fire; the face warms as the light grows. Source: Sahasranama verses 1 and 2 ("born of the fire-pit of consciousness", "a thousand rising suns"; [Sahasranama text](https://www.radhakrishnatemple.net/blog/lalitha-sahasranamam-lyrics-and-meaning-2/)) and the Lalitopakhyana emergence ([Vedadhara](https://www.vedadhara.com/tripura-sundari)). Face and light only; the fire is optional and per-anchor.
2. **The smile that overwhelms the Lord of Desire.** A slow, restrained smile forms, starting at the eyes, a faint head tilt as if toward someone she loves. Source: Sahasranama verse 11, "her gentle smile enraptures Kameshwara" (name 28 per Wikipedia), and "eyes full of waves of compassion" in the dhyana verse 2 ([Sahasranama page](https://www.radhakrishnatemple.net/blog/lalitha-sahasranamam-lyrics-and-meaning-2/)).
3. **The glance that gives life back.** Her stern or still expression softens into compassion and her gaze turns toward the viewer, as when she restores Kama at Rati's plea. Source: Lalitopakhyana ch. 30, resurrection of Madana ([hindupedia](https://www.hindupedia.com/en/Lalitop%C4%81khy%C4%81na)); the "smiled with compassion and looked at Kameshwara, and Manmatha took rebirth" telling is secondary, so mark it devotional.
4. **Calm sovereign after the storm.** After the battle with Bhandasura, her expression moves from a steady, level, unhurried look to quiet serenity, shoulders easing, head lifting slightly (eye level, not tilted up). Source: Lalitopakhyana war narrative and the four-day battle ending ([Wikipedia: Bhandasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhandasura)); no battle imagery needed, the beat is the face after victory.
5. **The image that would not leave.** A single slow step to a place, then rooted and still, as the Tripura Matabari image fixed itself to the ground at first light and the temple was built there. Source: Dhanya Manikya legend ([sacredsites.com](https://sacredsites.com/asia/india/tripura_sundari.html)); devotional, temple tradition only, and its "first rays of the sun" detail conflicts with the night rule (leave the sun out).

## Not found (do not invent)

- A **dhyana verse beginning "udyadbhanu"** tied to the Brahmanda Purana as a standalone dhyana: found only as Sahasranama name verse 2, not a separate meditation verse.
- A full scriptural **Panchadashi/Shodashi dhyana** in the Tantra with exact words for her seat, hands and dress in one verse: only the Sahasranama dhyana verses on a third-party page were seen; their attribution (the eight speech goddesses vs Dattatreya or Shankara) is not settled.
- **Any story of Tripura Sundari at night, in moonlight, or at a cremation ground.** None found; the ritual fire scene is the only dark-adjacent setting.
- A text or source for **"embodies sixteen types of desire"**.
- A **Hemalekha** story (a name raised in the brief for Tripura Rahasya): not found; the Jnana Khanda speakers are Hemaprabha, a renunciant woman and Ashtaka.
- A **Bhaskararaya** biography or a meeting with Dikshitar.
- Whether **Kamakshi appears in the Kama revival scene**: not found.
- A **Kinsley** chapter text on her iconography (book exists, "She Who Is Lovely in the Three Worlds", not accessed).
- A verified **chapter and verse reference** for most Lalitopakhyana beats (the Tagare translation is online but the PDF was not readable here).
- Independent confirmation of the **Tripura Upanishad (16 verses)** claim, the **Pancha Brahmas** reading and the **Sri Chakra sixteen-petal** enclosure beyond one line each.
- Any ground for **Tibetan Kurukulla** being the same goddess: one Tibetan-art page notes a shared attribute set; no more.
