# Tara (Mahavidya 2): research dossier

Status: **draft for human review**. Built from search-result summaries on 2026-10-08 (full pages were not opened). Sources disagree in places; disagreements are marked.

## Who she is

- Second of the ten Mahavidyas; a tantric form of Adishakti / Parvati. Closely related to Kali, and the two are often confused.
- **Name:** "Tara" is read three ways: *star* (a guide for sailors in the night), *saviour*, and *the one who ferries across* the ocean of worldly existence (samsara).
- **Purpose for devotees:** guidance and crossing over suffering, protection from sudden danger, and knowledge.
- **Named forms:** *Ugra Tara* (protects from severe dangers), *Nila Saraswati* (blue; gives knowledge and speech; Brihad Nila Tantra), *Ekajata* (single braid of hair).

## Appearance (sources agree unless noted)

- **Complexion:** blue (light to dark blue depending on source); Kali is black. Three eyes.
- **Hair:** matted into a sadhu-style jata, or a single braid (Ekajata). *Our locked anchor has loose flowing hair, which departs from this.*
- **Dress:** tiger-skin wrap held by a belt (Kali is shown naked; this is a main difference).
- **Ornaments:** gold, pearls and jewels; a snake around the neck or body; a garland of skulls or severed heads (said to stand for the letters of the alphabet); bone ornaments at the forehead. Often depicted pot-bellied.
- **Hand objects (sources disagree):** scissors (cutting attachment), skull cup, sword or scimitar, blue lotus, knife. Sets vary by source.
- **Posture:** commonly standing over a supine corpse, often Shiva, in a wrathful, dancing stance with legs apart.

## Settings and scenes

- **Cremation ground** is her classical home. Tantric practitioners hold she is drawn to bones; Tarapith (West Bengal) is a riverside cremation ground and Shakti Peetha where, in one account, Sati's eye (or third eye) fell.
- **The ocean** is her other setting, through the churning of the ocean legend and the "ocean of existence" imagery.
- Standing on a corpse or on Shiva is the standard iconographic scene.

## Myths and relationships

- **The poison story (devotional tradition, not in the main Puranic accounts):** Shiva drank the poison from the churning of the ocean and collapsed. Tara appeared, took him in her lap and nursed him; her milk calmed the burning of the poison. (One variant says she took the poison herself; some versions name Durga or Parvati.) The Tarapith sanctum has an old stone image of Tara nursing Shiva.
- **Bamakhepa (1837 to 1911), saint of Tarapith:** devoted to Tara and called her mother. After he was beaten for eating the offering first, she reportedly appeared in a queen's dream and told her to feed him first because he was her son.
- Characters she relates to: Shiva (as the one she saves), devotees she calls her children, and the sage Vasishtha (a legend linked to Tarapith).

## Cautions

- Tourism and hotel sites made claims (offerings, blessings) not confirmed by scholarly sources; ignored.
- No source found for "guide speech". Her link to speech and knowledge is via *Nila Saraswati*.
- Hand-object sets, the poison story's identity of the goddess, and the etymology all vary by source.

## Implications for the anchor and the video

- The locked anchor already matches: blue skin, third eye, tiger-skin pattern, skull garland, serpent ornaments. It omits the held objects, and the hair is loose, not matted.
- Her story is *crossing over, protection and rescue*, not standing still and raising a hand. A beat should show her saving or guiding.

## Candidate beats (non-graphic)

1. **Calming the poisoned sea (recommended):** dark water churns with faint dark currents; She extends Her open hand, the water settles, and a path of starlight forms toward the viewer. Echoes the poison and ocean-crossing story.
2. **Cutting attachment:** She holds the scissors and slowly closes them on a glowing thread drifting from the dark water; the cut thread dissolves into stars. Directly iconographic (scissors cut attachment).
3. **Nursing Shiva:** central to her tradition, but not recommended for this account (sensitive and hard to render reverently).

## Additional research (round 2)

- **"Tara" means star and also pupil of the eye.** A Sanskrit dictionary lists both senses ([Cologne Sanskrit lexicon](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MW72Scan/MW72Scanpdf/pg_0371.pdf)); star is the leading sense, pupil is a genuine secondary sense. The Bengali word *tara* means eyeball.
- **Tarapith is named for the eye.** Temple tradition holds that Sati's third eye (some say an eyeball) fell at the site, which is why the town became Tarapith ([Shubh Panchang](https://shubhpanchang.com/temples/tarapith-temple)). Sources vary between "third eye" and "eyeball".
- **Her name also reads as "to carry through"**, a root emphasising that she carries the soul across the ocean of suffering.
- **Scissors** are read (interpretively) as cutting away what is unnecessary, attachment and ego; **the blue lotus** as wisdom (a modern reading; it is the utpala, a standard attribute) ([Hindu Blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2025/01/symbolism-in-image-of-goddess-tara-dasa.html)).
- **Origin story (devotional):** the sage Vasishtha's early worship fails; he succeeds through kaula rites in Mahacina and the goddess becomes known as Mahachina Tara. Treat as a devotional retelling.
- **Correction to earlier work:** no textual source describes Tara as a guide for sailors; that image is a modern comparison (for example Stella Maris). The grounded readings are *star*, *saviour* and *carries across*.

### Beat directions that fit the video model
- **The eye and the star:** her name means star and the pupil of the eye; the third eye is the seat of the Tarapith legend. A face-centred beat with a slow push-in, where the third eye lights softly and one star above the crown brightens, uses the strengths of the model and the tradition.

## Stories and story seeds (round 3 research)

Reference image update: the crown carries a golden **sun disc** (not a crescent moon), with a bright star above it. She stands in shallow dark water.

### Stories found
- **The mother who answers a devoted son (Tarapith).** The saint Bamakhepa ate food before it was offered and was beaten. That night Tara appeared in a queen's dream and told her to feed him first because he was her son. [Wikipedia: Bamakhepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamakhepa), [Tarapith temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapith_temple).
- **Vasishtha asks her to appear as a mother** nursing Shiva; she took that form and became the stone image at Tarapith (devotional legend). [Tarapith (Wikipedia)](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Tarapith).
- **The two sisters (Tara Tarini, Odisha).** A childless Brahmin, Basu Praharaj, prayed for years. Two girls came to live with him as his daughters, then vanished; he followed them up the hill, where they revealed themselves as Tara and Tarini, forms of Adi Shakti, not his children. [Tara Tarini (sacredsites.com)](https://sacredsites.com/asia/india/taratarini_temple_information.html), [Live History India](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/monuments/taratarini-goddess-of-the-eastern-seas).
- **Patron of seafarers.** At Taratarini she was the patron of the merchants and seafarers of ancient Kalinga, who prayed to her before voyages; in the Pala period she is described as a goddess of navigation (that source concerns Tara more broadly, not only the Hindu Mahavidya). No folk tale of her rescuing a specific ship was found. [Live History India](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/monuments/taratarini-goddess-of-the-eastern-seas).
- **Vasishtha's first failure.** Vasishtha could not worship her successfully until he learned a different rite in Mahacina (Rudrayamala, devotional retelling). [Wikipedia: Tara (Mahavidya)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_(Mahavidya)).

### Iconographic action (Nila Tantra and Tantrasara verses, secondary summaries)
- She stands in the **pratyalidha** stance (an archer's stride) over a corpse, advancing with force; three fierce, bright eyes; a **terrifying laugh**; skull bowl, scissors and sword. [Himalayan Academy](https://www.himalayanacademy.com/view/c23_rajam), [mahavidya.ca](https://mahavidya.ca/2017/12/27/tara-the-goddess-who-guides-us-through-troubles/).
- **Three forms:** Ekajata (grants unity with the Absolute), Ugra Tara (relieves unforeseen severe suffering), Nila Saraswati (gives knowledge and speech). [Wikipedia: Tara (Mahavidya)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_(Mahavidya)).

### Not found (do not invent)
- No source for Tara killing the demon Hayagriva; no folk tale of her saving a merchant's ship; nothing on a "living goddess" gaze tradition.

### Story seeds for the Director (each beat must name one)
1. **Fierce protector, tender mother:** the Ugra gaze that guards you softens for the one who calls (the "feed him first, he is my son" story).
2. **The reveal:** the two sisters who turn out to be the goddess; the ordinary or hidden becoming plainly divine.
3. **Before the voyage:** the goddess the seafarer prays to before crossing; she looks out over the dark water and clears the way.
4. **The advancing stride (pratyalidha):** She steps toward you through the water with the unstoppable force the verses describe.
5. **The mother of the devoted:** She turns toward the viewer, recognising them as Her own.
